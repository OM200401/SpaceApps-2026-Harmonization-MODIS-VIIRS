Two satellites, one fire record

MODIS (since 2000) and VIIRS (since 2012) count fires differently. VIIRS sees far more small fires, so joined naively, fire seems to explode in 2012. We convert MODIS into VIIRS units so every year compares fairly.

The problem, and the fix ·

Raw totals jump in 2012; harmonized totals do not.

MODIS alone is one consistent instrument across both years, so its change is the real change in fire.

How the conversion works

1

Learn from clean overlap years

Both satellites flew together from 2012, but the MODIS satellites' orbits drift after 2021, so we learn only from June 2012 to 2021. We drop days when any satellite was down, and gas flares that the fire data counts as vegetation fires.

2

Convert with one worldwide model

In every 1° area (about 100 km), each month and day or night, a model learns how many VIIRS detections each MODIS detection is worth. It looks at how hot and how spread out the fires were, the other half of the day, the months around it and the neighbouring areas. Where MODIS saw nothing, it adds the small fires VIIRS usually finds there.

3

Join the records

More checks
Both methods were scored on the same cleaned data, each area over its whole box. Fitting on 2012–2018 and predicting 2019–2021 gives similar numbers (Canada 6.7%, world 1.7%). With only Terra (as in 2000–2002), the world error is 2.7% against 6.4% for the previous Terra ratio. For comparison, predicting S-NPP from NOAA-20, an identical VIIRS instrument, gives 1–4% per region: that is the best any conversion could do.

What to keep in mind
  • Before 2012 is an estimate. MODIS misses many small fires that VIIRS sees. Where MODIS saw nothing we add what VIIRS usually finds there, so single months in quiet places can be well off.
  • Megafire months are the hardest. In huge fires (NWT 2014, British Columbia 2018) smoke hides fire from MODIS, and those months can come out 10–25% low.
  • Outside the tested regions, accuracy varies. The whole world is converted with the same model, but sparse places (the Arctic, deserts, small islands) are much less certain.
  • 2000–2002 are less certain. Only the Terra satellite was flying. We tested this by hiding Aqua in 2012–2021, but the satellite itself was younger then.
  • Gas flares are left out. Spots that burn all year, mostly at night (oil and gas flares), are removed from every year, so some oil-field areas show less than the raw data.
  • The latest months are provisional. They use NASA's near-real-time data, which is replaced by the final archive a few months later.
  • A detection is not a fire. One large fire can produce hundreds of hotspot detections. Counts measure burning activity, not the number of separate fires.
  • Small areas are noisier. Error grows as the area you draw gets smaller (about 25% for a single 1° box).

Data and code
  • NASA FIRMS active fire detections: MODIS Collection 6.1 (Terra and Aqua) and VIIRS 375 m S-NPP Collection 2, via NASA Earthdata. Vegetation fires only, nominal and high confidence.
  • Counted per 0.25° grid cell (about 25 km) and month, by satellite and day or night.
  • Full method and accuracy numbers for the model shown here: the v2 method notes. The method we used before it, with its validation: VALIDATION.md. All the code is in the GitHub repository.
